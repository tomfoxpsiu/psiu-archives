William Smith Clark

Wikipedia ↗
  • ChapterGamma
  • Class of1848
  • InstitutionAmherst College
  • FieldEducation

William Smith Clark was an American scholar of chemistry, botany, and zoology, a Union Army colonel during the Civil War, and a major figure in the development of agricultural education in both the United States and Japan. After graduating from Amherst College in 1848, he earned a doctorate in chemistry from the University of Göttingen in 1852. Clark then spent fifteen years teaching chemistry at Amherst before joining the 21st Massachusetts Volunteer Infantry, eventually rising to the rank of colonel. In 1867, he became the third president of the Massachusetts Agricultural College-today the University of Massachusetts Amherst. His leadership attracted the attention of Japanese officials, who invited him to help establish the Sapporo Agricultural College (now Hokkaido University). There, he introduced modern agricultural teaching and research methods and helped create Japan's first agricultural experiment station, a cornerstone of the country's modernization of farming and crop development.
