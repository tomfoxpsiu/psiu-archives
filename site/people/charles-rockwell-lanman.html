Charles Rockwell Lanman

Wikipedia ↗
  • ChapterBeta
  • Class of1871
  • InstitutionYale University
  • FieldWriters & Publishers

Charles Lanman was an American scholar of Sanskrit who wrote the widely used Sanskrit Reader (1884) and helped edit the "Harvard Oriental Series," which offered scholarly English translations of the ancient Hindu Vedic texts. After receiving his doctorate from Yale University and receiving additional education in Germany, Lanman was appointed professor at Johns Hopkins University in1876 and later at Harvard University in 1880, where he became Wales professor of Sanskrit from 1903 to 1926. In 1891, he became editor of the Harvard Oriental Series, founded and funded by his student and friend Henry Clark Warren. Under Lanman's leadership, the series produced 31 volumes, including landmark English translations such as Whitney's Atharva Veda and Arthur B. Keith's editions of the Yajur Veda and Rig Veda Brahmanas. Lanman also acquired roughly 500 Sanskrit and Prakrit books and manuscripts for Harvard which, alongside those eventually donated by philologist Fitzedward Hall, make up the most valuable collection of its kind in America.
