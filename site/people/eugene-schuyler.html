Eugene Schuyler

Wikipedia ↗
  • ChapterBeta
  • Class of1859
  • InstitutionYale University
  • FieldPolitics

was an American scholar, author, explorer, and diplomat. Among the first three Americans to earn a Ph.D. from a U.S. university, he also became the first American to translate the works of major Russian writers Ivan Turgenev and Leo Tolstoy. Schuyler served as an American consul in Moscow and was the first U.S. diplomat to travel through Russian Central Asia. While serving as Consul General in Istanbul, he played a pivotal role in exposing the 1876 Turkish atrocities in Bulgaria during the April Uprising. He went on to serve as the first American Minister to both Romania and Serbia, and later as U.S. Minister to Greece. Upon the 1901 posthumous publication of his essays and memoirs, a review in the New York Times described Schuyler as "one of America's most brilliant scholars, patriots, and men of letters."
