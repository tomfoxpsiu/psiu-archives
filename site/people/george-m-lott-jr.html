George M. Lott Jr.

Look up on Wikipedia ↗
  • ChapterOmega
  • Class of1928
  • InstitutionUniversity of Chicago
  • FieldAthletics

George Lott was a member of the International Tennis Hall of Fame. He was a two-time Wimbledon doubles champ, played on five Davis Cup squads, and won numerous American titles. He is widely considered one of the greatest doubles players of his era. In singles, Lott reached the semifinals of the U.S. National Championships in 1928 and advanced to the final in 1931, where he lost to Ellsworth Vines. He also made history as the first player-later joined by only three others-to win the Cincinnati Open four times (1924, 1925, 1927, and 1932). Between 1928 and 1934, Lott secured five U.S. National Championships doubles titles with three different partners. His doubles success extended internationally, with victories at Wimbledon in 1931 and 1934, the French Championships in 1931, and a total of four Grand Slam titles. Lott was inducted into the International Tennis Hall of Fame in 1964. After his playing career, Lott served as a tennis coach at DePaul University from 1969 to 1991.
