George Bird Grinnell

Wikipedia ↗
  • ChapterBeta
  • Class of1870
  • InstitutionYale University
  • FieldPolitics

Geroge Grinnell was an American anthropologist, historian, naturalist, and author whose influence helped shape early conservation in the United States. A leading advocate for Native American rights and for protecting the wildlife of the American West, he played a central role in shifting public opinion and legislation toward preserving the American bison. Grinnell was also editor of and president of Forest and Stream - the leading natural history magazine in North America - the founder of the Audubon Society and the Boone and Crockett Club, an organizer of the New York Zoological Society and an advisor to President Theodore Roosevelt. Glacier National Park in Montana came about largely through his efforts, and the park's Mount Grinnell is named after him.
