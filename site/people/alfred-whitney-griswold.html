Alfred Whitney Griswold

Wikipedia ↗
  • ChapterBeta
  • Class of1929
  • InstitutionYale University
  • FieldEducation

Alfred Griswold was an American historian, professor, and the 16th president of Yale University. During his presidency from 1951 to 1963, he oversaw the expansion of Yale's modern scientific research infrastructure, especially on Science Hill. He tripled the university's endowment to $375 million, added 26 new buildings, and created research fellowships for young scholars, with a strong emphasis on the sciences. Griswold also wrote several influential works on foreign policy and education, including The Far Eastern Policy of the United States (1938), Farming and Democracy (1948), Essays on Education (1954), In the University Tradition (1957), and Liberal Education and the Democratic Ideal (1959). He received honorary degrees from a dozen universities, including Harvard in 1950.
