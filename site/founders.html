The seven founders

Seven Union College undergraduates — four in the class of 1836, three in the class of 1837 — met in an attic room in downtown Schenectady and signed a formal pledge to found a society of their own. They were between sixteen and twenty-three years old. Six of them lived long enough to be asked what had happened, and their answers do not agree.

These seven biographies were written from the archive itself — from the 1884 Epitome, the 1941 Annals, Fiske's 1895 history and the earliest numbers of The Diamond, in which the founders were still writing about themselves. Every claim carries the volume and page it came from, and where the sources disagree — and on the founding they disagree a great deal — the page says so rather than choosing. 84 open questions are listed across the seven, each with a note on where a researcher could settle it. What is still missing is the physical record: not one burial place appears anywhere in the archive, and six of the seven have no portrait. Corrections and additions go in data/founders.xlsx — see Filling in the Founders and the Timeline.
