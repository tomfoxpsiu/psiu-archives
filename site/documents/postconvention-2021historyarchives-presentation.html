History and Archives Topic: The Rho (1896)

Slide 8

University of Wisconsin Founded in Madison 1848
Psi U held a convention in Madison in 1851
April 30, 1851 - the Convention of Alumni in Wisconsin sent a letter, written by Albert C. Ingham, Theta '47, from Madison, Wis., to the Theta. (This letter is apparently in the archives)
"Now then the Psi U Convention of Wisconsin ask a Chapter for this University. We propose applying for it at the next general convention in July and we think our claims are good. "
1852 - 1853 - Similar Wisconsin Conventions were held in the years 1852 and 1853; and with out success annual petitions were dispatched to the annual Conventions.
Psi U and University of Wisconsin before the Rho
Notes: "The application, though so strongly supported, was not granted. Our Fraternity, then confined to Eastern colleges, and uncertain about the new State Universities, resolved to wait." Albert P. Jacobs, Phi '73, writing in the 1896 Psi Upsilon Review.

Slide 9

1875 - Phi Kappa Psi Chapter founded at Madison.
1892 - Charles Kendall Adams, Phi '61, called to the presidency of the University of Wisconsin.
1892 - Two groups including the Phi Kappa Psi chapter approached Brother Adams about petitioning Psi Upsilon.
As Charles Spooner, Rho '94, wrote later, "Dr. Adams was very kind but told me that, as president of the university, he personally could do nothing about the matter. But he did put me in touch with a Detroit Psi U with whom I might correspond. . ."
1894 - the Phi Kappa Psi chapter formally withdrew to become the Rho Kappa Upsilon local fraternity
Psi U and University of Wisconsin before the Rho
Notes: (Trivia: Prior to that posting, Charles Adams succeeded Andrew D. White, Beta '53, as President of Cornell)
Historical Note: it was not uncommon for members to be in "Junior societies" and join other societies. One of the leaders of Pi Kappa Psi was Charles P. Spooner '94, initiated as a Psi U at Princeton, when he transferred schools.

Slide 10

Context question of the late 1800's: How should Psi Upsilon expand
Without any question, he knew more about college fraternities of every description than any man in America….He believed the Wisconsin matter was certain of eventual success, provided (a) the local society stood up as a group of men and (b) it gave every promise of being a permanent institution. He based his opinion upon the fact that the two most Western chapters, the Phi and the Mu, were altogether too far apart geographically to be of any use to each other so far as association was concerned. In addition to this, he states in one of his letters that at the time the Mu was seeking admission he found several chapters of Psi Upsilon which preferred Wisconsin to Minnesota because of the distance between Ann Arbor and Minneapolis.
Mr.Bangs was just as remarkable a man as Mr. Jacobs, but in an entirely different way. He was thoroughly Eastern and thoroughly New York City. I do not believe that he ever voluntarily gave his consent to the extension of the Fraternity into the Middle West. He felt that the East with its non-coeducational and private type of old institutions in which Psi Upsilon had so many strong chapters was, generally speaking, the region to which it ought to confine its activities. He did not like the idea of extending the Fraternity to State and, necessarily, coeducational institutions. He was afraid that sooner or later mistakes would be made by such expansion and that the Fraternity would be likely in any such event to become cumbersome and difficult to manage.

Slide 11

Petitioning - 1894-1895
The vote of the Convention favored the petition fourteen to six. Charles P. Spooner continues his story: A large part of this was attributable to that amazing man, Albert Poole Jacobs, and the work he had done. Credit must also be given to the favorable attitude and the strong support given us by the Phi chapter at the University of Michigan. At times, however, the petitioners became discouraged, even though they were fully cognizant of Psi Upsilon's policy of conservatism. A local society must struggle for existence.

Slide 21

The Response from Phi Kappa Psi
The Grand Arch Council of Phi Kappa Psi held its session at Cleveland, April 8, 9, and 10th, 1896.

The principal point of interest of the second day's session was a denouncement of the action of the Psi Upsilon fraternity in giving a charter to the Rho Kappa Upsilon society at the University of Wisconsin, which was the outgrowth of the Alpha Chapter of the Phi Kappa Psi fraternity.

Slide 22

The Response from Phi Kappa Psi (cont.)
"Whereas, the Phi Kappa Psi fraternity in Grand Arch Convention assembled, have learned of the absorption of the local society known as the Rho Kappa Upsilon society of the University of Wisconsin by the Psi Upsilon fraternity on the 27th day of March, 1896, and
Whereas, in our judgement the organization of the Rho Kappa Upsilon Society by the attempted irregular withdrawal of the members from the Wisconsin Alpha Chapter of Phi Kappa Psi fraternity was a result of a conspiracy between the members of the Psi Upsilon fraternity and the Wisconsin Chapter of Phi Kappa Psi, and
Whereas, such a conspiracy is an act of a character which has always been deemed unworthy of any reputable fraternity, and thus injurious to the reputation and standing of intercollegiate fraternities in the estimation of the public and college world, now therefore be it
Resolved, by the Phi Kappa Psi fraternity in Grand Arch Council assembled, that it hereby denounces as literally contemptible the conduct of the Psi Upsilon fraternity, and earnestly calls the attention of the college and fraternity world to the same; and to the fact that would now appear to be part of the avowed policy of the Psi Upsilon fraternity to carry on the illegitimate warfare of this character, and be it further
Resolved, That we earnestly recommend that in all future inter-fraternity intercourse the treatment to be accorded the Psi Upsilon fraternity should be such as is consistent with their deliberate conduct and avowed policy on its part."

Slide 24

The Rho Owl Song - Charles McClure '95
The "Rho Owl Song" was written by Brother Charles McClure, Rho '95, and dedicated to the chapter and was sung at the initiation banquet.
Set to the tune of a popular song that had also been a Yale marching song
Brother McClure was a renowned lyricist who wrote many songs for the chapter.
The text of the song ·is symbolic of the hardships experienced by Rho Kappa Upsilon during the three years it was striving to make Psi Upsilon and shows up the petty back-biting of the defamers of Rho Kappa and the manner in which glorious old Psi U. ignored them.

This song was well explained by Brother Charles H. Bernard, Rho '95, in the Pi Garnet of February, 1939: "Hurt by the decision of the Wisconsin chapter to surrender its charter and petition for membership in Psi Upsilon, Phi Kappa Psi and similar organizations, termed by the Phi chapter at the University of Michigan as 'small fry societies,' attempted to influence existing chapters of Psi U to refuse the Wisconsin petitioners a charter." In the Rho Owl song, the owl 'who dwelt in great exclusiveness' represents Psi Upsilon, and the 'lion bold who rampant awoke one morn awakening consternation in the landwhere he was born' is the Wisconsin chapter which renounced Phi Kappa Psi to seek admission. Phi Kappa Psi and other on-campus fraternities are the 'envious jackals' who asked Psi Upsilon to 'bar from out thy portals fair this traitor double-dyed.' "The song describes in graphic detail the attempt on the part of Phi Kappa Psi to oust Psi U. petitioners from campus offices and the assistance rendered what was to become our Rho chapter by Chi Psi, the other leading chapter on the Wisconsin campus in 1893. "The paean of praise which the lion sings to the 'ancient owl' Psi Upsilon-on being received into the fraternity contains the lasting loyalty and devotion to the fraternity which has always been remarkably strong in the Rho chapter."

Slide 35

The fire of 1918
On a subzero night in January of 1918 this new house burned to the ground. Scantily clad boys of the chapter scurried to safety without casualties. But a temporary place had to be rented at 25 Langdon Street until the house was rebuilt, this time in a more fireproof manner. Early in the following school year it was ready for occupancy and has been the home of the Rho since. Title to its rests in Rho of Psi Upsilon, Incorporated, an alumni association modeled almost exactly after the corporate structure of the Delta.
