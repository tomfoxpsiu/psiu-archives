Slide 7

A quick note:
There are many many MANY books written about these men. This presentation is to present Psi U connections and trivia, to lay to rest some myths, and to do so in a brief and entertaining manner.
We would like to recommend all brothers read about these titans who strove to reach the highest moral, intellectual, and social excellence and to use those skills for the betterment of society. They are each fascinating in their own ways.

Slide 8

Chester A. Arthur, Theta 1848 (Union College) - Arthur attended Union College in 1845 where he studied the traditional classics. As a senior there in 1848, at age 18, he was elected to Phi Beta Kappa and president of the debate society.
Chester Alan Arthur was elected vice president of the United States in the election of 1880, as James Garfield's running mate. He became president following Garfield's assassination that same year. He served as president until 1885.
Chester A. Arthur, Theta 1848 (Union)

Slide 9

Chester A. Arthur - early life
Born in Fairfield, Vermont, October 5, 1829. Named after the doctor that delivered him.
At Union College, he helped throw the school bell into the Erie Canal as a prank.
Arthur's college friend, Silas Burt, said that Chet-as his friends called him-"could carry a great deal of wine and liquor without any manifest effect other than greater variety of speech."
During the Civil War, he served the Union as inspector-general and then quartermaster, in charge of providing equipment, clothing, and supplies to those troops in New York.
He was a staunch abolitionist. When 24-years-old he successfully represented Lizzie Jennings, who was forcibly removed from a streetcar in 1854 because of her skin color. The day after a jury awarded Jennings $225.00 in damages, the Third Avenue Railway Company had its streetcars desegregated.

Slide 10

Garfield was shot July 2, 1881 and died several weeks later. Arthur became a reluctant President and was sworn in to the office in New York City.
Arthur pushed thru the Pendleton Act which required the federal government to award jobs on the basis of merit - ending the spoils system of political patronage. This earned Arthur the moniker "The Father of Civil Service."
He had no vice president for his entire four-year term. The 25th Amendment would not be passed until 1967.
His Secretary of War was Robert Lincoln, son of President Abraham Lincoln.
He did not campaign vigorously for reelection. He was aware-but kept secret-that he had a terminal illness (Bright's disease) and would most likely not live through a second term.
Chester A. Arthur - political life
During the time of political bosses and the 'spoils' system, he was a compromise selection. He was Garfield's running mate and became Vice President after they won the election.
Chester and Garfield did not see eye-to-eye while in office. It has been written that Garfield wouldn't even let him into the White House.

Slide 11

Chester A. Arthur - fun facts
Was a member of the Delphian Society while at Union.
Nicknames include: "The Gentleman Boss"; "His Accidency"; "Elegant Arthur"; "The Dude President"
Arthur had anything in the White House that wasn't nailed down carted away and sold at public auction to raise money for new furniture. The 24 wagon-loads included a pair of Abraham Lincoln's pants and John Quincy Adams' hats. He commissioned Louis Tiffany to redecorate the White House.
He was the first president to have a personal valet; he owned at least 80 pairs of pants. He engaged Louis Comfort Tiffany to renovate the White House.
Arthur dedicated the landmark Washington Monument in DC on February 21, 1885.

Slide 12

Chester A. Arthur - fun facts
In 1884, the International Meridian Conference was held at President Arthur's behest. This established the Greenwich Meridian which is still in use today.
Two towns and one county in Nebraska are named after him-Arthur, the country seat of Arthur County, and Chester. Also, Lake Arthur in Minnesota.
Arthur was President of the Psi Upsilon Association of Washington, DC, and the father of Chester Alan Arthur, Jr, Lambda '85.
Brother Arthur has no living descendants.

Slide 13

Chester A. Arthur - his legacy
Died in New York City November 18, 1886, aged only 57 years. There is a monument to him in New York City at Madison and East 26th St.
Publisher Alexander K. McClure wrote, "No man ever entered the Presidency so profoundly and widely distrusted, and no one ever retired… more generally respected."
Mark Twain, deeply cynical about politicians, conceded, "It would be hard indeed to better President Arthur's administration."
Lies at rest in an elaborate tomb near Albany, NY.☞

Slide 14

Alphonso Taft; Not a Psi U, but…
Father of William Howard Taft Likely the reason his sons and grandsons were dedicated Psi Us. Alphonso Taft - former U.S. Attorney General, and distinguished judge. Secretary of War in 1876. He taught school to earn money to attend Amherst Academy. During his time at Amherst, he and Samuel Colt stole a cannon belonging to General Ebenezer Mattoon and shot it at their school. Taft entered Yale College in 1829 and he graduated four years later in 1833. Taft helped create the secret society known as Skull and Bones in 1832 with William Huntington Russell.Taft was appointed as minister to Austria-Hungary by Chester A. Arthur in 1882. He served until July 4, 1884, and was then transferred by President Arthur to Minister of Russia, and he served in St. Petersburg until August 1885. Taft had a reputation for serving political office with integrity and character. Taft was a rare and modern 19th politician who supported black voting rights and who reduced government corruption while holding office.

Slide 15

A true "man for all seasons," Taft was both a brilliant politician and an avid Psi U. In the course of his illustrious career he served as president of the United States, chief justice of the Supreme Court (the only man to hold both of these positions), governor general of the Philippines, and secretary of war. It was under his guidance that the Supreme Court moved from the Capitol into a building of its own, greatly adding to the stature of the Court.
He was one of the prime motivating forces behind Psi Upsilon's growth into an international organization as it was largely through his urging that, in 1920, Psi Upsilon established the Nu chapter at the University of Toronto.
William Howard Taft, Beta 1878 (Yale)

Slide 16

William Howard Taft - early life
W.H. Taft was born on September 15, 1857, in Cincinnati, Ohio

He was a heavyweight wrestling champion at Yale, standing about 6 feet tall and weighed 243 pounds when he graduated from college.
He famously struggled with his weight and may have weighed more than
330 pounds as President, but he was at his college weight at the time of
his death.
Career goal was to be a Supreme Court justice; Taft was Solicitor General in the Harrison
administration at 32, and a federal circuit judge at 34. He remains the only solicitor general
to be president.

Slide 17

He was Governor of the Philippines when the United States gained control over the Philippines after the conclusion of the Spanish-American war, and subsequently served as Secretary of War under President Theodore Roosevelt. Despite his initial hesitancy about it, Taft's service in the Philippines from 1900 to 1903 was fulfilling and largely successful.
Taft - political life
While there, he twice turned down President Roosevelt's offer of a Supreme Court appointment in order to finish his work. Becoming governor of the islands in 1901, Taft abandoned tactics the U.S. military government had used and by the time he left the islands to become Roosevelt's secretary of war in 1903, Taft had constructed a functioning civil regime and pacified the islands.
Taft and James Monroe were the only two Presidents to serve as Secretary of War.

Slide 19

William Howard Taft - political life
Presidency - Characterized by trust-busting, strengthening the Interstate Commerce Commission, expanding the civil service, establishing a better postal system, and promoting world peace. Pursued "dollar diplomacy"- the policy itself was aimed at furthering the interests of the U.S. abroad by encouraging the investment of U.S. capital in foreign countries, specifically, Latin and South America. Roosevelt broke with Taft in 1911, charging Taft was too reactionary. Taft and the conservatives were alarmed at Roosevelt's attacks on the judiciary, and took control of the party machinery. Roosevelt opposed his renomination in 1912 and bolted from the Republican Party to form his own "Bull-Moose" party. Taft defeated Roosevelt for the Republican nomination in a bruising battle in 1912 that forced Roosevelt out of the GOP and left Taft's people in charge for decades. William Howard Taft remains the only U.S. President to finish third in a bid for reelection. Supreme Court - Upon leaving the White House in 1913, Taft was appointed the Chancellor Kent Professor of Law and Legal History at Yale Law School. Upon his appointment, the Yale Chapter of the Acacia Fraternity made him an honorary member. At the same time Taft was elected president of the American Bar Association.
Taft taught at Yale Law School until he was named by President Warren Harding to the Supreme Court in 1921. He was a supreme court justice and chief justice for about 10 years.
While Chief Justice, Taft wrote the opinion for the Court in 256 cases out of the Court's ever-growing caseload. His philosophy of constitutional interpretation was essentially historical contextualism.
Notes: As secretary of war, Taft became Roosevelt's chief emissary and confidant, assisting him in the Portsmouth Peace negotiations, and in establishing a protectorate in Cuba. Roosevelt, having sworn upon his victory in 1904 that he would not seek another term, handpicked Taft to succeed him in 1908. The public joked that T.A.F.T. stood for "take advice from Theodore." Thanks in part to Roosevelt's popularity, Taft's victory over Democrat William Jennings Bryan was decisive. Taft promised to continue Roosevelt's reform program. But Roosevelt, and many of his allies, saw Taft's administration as abandoning progressivism. The consequent animosity split the Republican Party in 1912, sweeping Woodrow Wilson into office.

Taft - fun facts
Called "Big Bill" by his brothers.
Taft was the first president to throw the first pitch of baseball season, beginning a tradition that continues today. The game was in 1910, between the Washington Senators and the Philadelphia Athletics. The Senators won 3-0. Taft was the last president to keep a cow at the White House to provide fresh milk. Her name was Pauline. Taft was the first president to own a car. He actually converted the White House stables into a garage!
Admitted 2 states to the Union; New Mexico and Arizona and appointed 6 Supreme Court justices After a fire burned much of the town of Moron, California during the 1920s, it was renamed Taft, in his honor. Taft is the only former president to swear in an incoming president. While serving as Chief Justice, Taft administered the Oath of Office to Calvin Coolidge in 1925 and Herbert Hoover in 1929.
Notes: Taft didn't just have a contentious relationship with the size of his bath, but fought a porcelain-fixing ring while president. These toilet and tub manufacturers had colluded to drive up prices and were known as the Bathtub Trust-a trust "broken" by President Taft.

Nellie Taft
The capital's famous Japanese cherry trees were planted around the Tidal Basin at the request of Helen "Nellie" Taft. The Taft years were famous for social events, the most outstanding was an evening garden party for several thousand guests on the Tafts' silver wedding anniversary, June 19, 1911.
President William Howard Taft and First Lady Helen Taft were huge automobile enthusiasts and the seven-passenger White Steamer was the favorite of the White House fleet and often tested on the paved "Speedway" in Potomac Park and along the river.
Inspired by fond memories of pleasant evening carriage rides through the tree-lined streets of Manila, First Lady Helen Taft's support for turning this "speedway" into a promenade led to the first public project ever undertaken by a first lady. A plan was formed and Mrs. Taft selected a site for a bandstand to hold Marine Band concerts in the park on Wednesday and Saturday afternoons.
Notes: As secretary of war, Taft became Roosevelt's chief emissary and confidant, assisting him in the Portsmouth Peace negotiations, and in establishing a protectorate in Cuba. William Howard Taft - Psi Upsilon
President Taft attended the next day celebrations after the 1910 initiation of the Xi. As he left the house he cried, "Long live the Xi!" An autographed picture from Brother William H. Taft hung in the Kappa house.
William Howard Taft (Beta '78), a former President of the United States, but not yet appointed Chief Justice, was one of two speakers at the 1915 convention hosted in University of California Hon. William H. Taft, "Hon. President of the PSI UPSILON ALUMNI ASSOCIATION OF MONTREAL" March 1926 1929 Convention was held in Washington in order to honor Brother Taft. Taft defied his doctor's recommendations in order to attend. The Convention of 1929 hosted in DC was the first with a host chapter, the Eta, away from its home campus. The Eta honored the former president and current Supreme Court Justice at a formal luncheon His convention address is well-worth the reading, available on PsiU.org "Three things", he said "will make [young Psi U Brothers] useful to themselves and to the world. One is work and hard work; another is self-denial; and a third is the discipline of character the practice of the others brings about."

A Psi U, Henry Lewis Stimson, Beta '88 was a Cabinet officer under four Presidents; Brother Stimson served as Secretary of War in the Cabinet of President William H. Taft, Beta '78, from May, 1911 to March, 1913; as Secretary of State in the Cabinet of President Hoover, 1929-33; Secretary of War in President Roosevelt's Cabinet, July, 1940-April, 1945; and in President Truman's Cabinet, April-September, 1945.
Gifford Pinchot, Beta '89, pioneer of American Forest conservation and Governor of Pennsylvania from 1923 to 1927, served under Taft as well.

Slide 27

Taft in the White House
President William Howard Taft also attempted to install a type of air conditioning in the expanded West Wing in 1909. The experimental system consisted of electric fans that blew over great bins of ice in the attic, cooling the air, which was forced through the air ducts of the heating system. This never worked well enough and was soon abandoned. Taft also built a sleeping porch, a screened-in room on the roof of the White House for a cool place to sleep amid breezes on summer evenings.
The stuck in a bathtub myth is just that; a myth. When he moved in, Taft had the residence bathrooms upgraded In fact, the tub was originally installed aboard the ship he took to oversee construction of the Panama Canal. An engineering periodical covered the tub, stating that though Taft enjoyed bathing, but he simply used a shower if his size was otherwise unaccommodated.
Taft didn't just have a contentious relationship with the size of his bath, but fought a porcelain-fixing ring while president. These toilet and tub manufacturers had colluded to drive up prices and were known as the Bathtub Trust-a trust "broken" by President Taft.

Slide 28

William Howard Taft - his legacy
On March 11, he became the first president[13] to be buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
After his death on March 8, 1930, Taft lay in state at the U.S. Capitol Rotunda for three days prior to his burial at Arlington. The Taft family selected a large plot in Section 30 of the cemetery, which at the time was relatively undeveloped. Taft requested a s imple ceremony with no eulogies, insisting instead upon poetry by Wadsworth and Tennyson. His burial service, too, was simple, although an elaborate procession from the Capitol preceded it, with a funeral escort provided by the 3rd Infanty Regiment.
"William Howard Taft, Beta '78, has been termed by Life Magazine "the outstanding career man of American history, successively United States Solicitor General, Circuit Judge, Governor of the Philippines, Secretary of War, President, Chief Justice."

Lies-at-rest in an elaborate tomb near Albany, NY.☞

Nelson A. Rockefeller, Zeta 1930 (Dartmouth College) - Like Averell Harriman, Rockefeller was not content to rest upon his inheritance and rose to make his mark on the United States. He began his career as the head of Rockefeller Center, Inc., which coordinated economic and cultural relations between the Americas.
He later went on to seek the governor's office in New York State, and ran for President three times (in 1960, 1964, and 1968)
The highlight of his political career came in 1974 when Gerald Ford named him to serve as Vice President of the United States.
Nelson A. Rockefeller, Zeta 1930

Slide 30

Nelson Aldrich Rockefeller, Zeta 19 - early life
Nelson Rockefeller was born to John Davison Rockefeller Jr. and Abigail Greene. He was the second child of six children by his parent. He had his elementary education at the Lincoln School. He continued the Dartmouth College and graduated with An A.B in economics. Whiles there, he was a member of the Casque and Gauntlet, Phi Beta Kappa and Zeta Chapter of the Psi Upsilon.
He was a grandson of one of America's wealthiest people, John D. Rockefeller, Sr., founder of the Standard Oil Company. Graduated from Dartmouth College in 1930 with a degree in economics, he spent the remainder of the decade working for a number of family enterprises, including Chase National (later Chase Manhattan) Bank, Rockefeller Center, and Creole Petroleum.

Slide 31

Nelson A. Rockefeller - political life
1940 coordinator of inter-American affairs at the State Department, rising to assistant secretary of state for Latin American affairs in 1944. From 1953 to 1955 Rockefeller served as undersecretary of the newly created Department of Health, Education, and Welfare. Ran for Governor of New York in 1958, reelected in 1962, 1966, and 1970. Rockefeller oversaw massive changes in New York's fiscal, cultural, and educational policies and facilities. The state university system was greatly expanded, and the number of state employees and the size of the budget doubled and quadrupled, respectively.

Slide 32

Nelson A. Rockefeller - VP
He was nominated to be Vice President by President Ford, who had assumed the presidency upon Nixon's resignation amid the Watergate scandal. After weeks of intense congressional hearings Rockefeller was confirmed by the House and Senate to the position on December 19, 1974.

Slide 34

Nelson A. Rockefeller - his legacy
Nelson Rockefeller died on January 26, 1978, from a fatal heart attack. Nelson A. Rockefeller Park is located in Battery Park City, NY.
Nelson Rockefeller won several awards including the Presidential Medal of Freedom, 1977, Distinguished Service to Conservation Award, National Wildlife Federation/Sears Roebuck Foundation, 1966, Gold Medal Award, National Institute of Social Sciences, 1967 (awarded to all five Rockefeller brothers), Distinguished Service Award, State University of New York, 1973, Order of Merit, Chile, 1945, National Order of the Southern Cross, Brazil, 1946, Agricultural Merit Award, Brazilian Rural Confederation, 1963, Grand Cordon of the Order of the Brilliant Star, Nationalist China, 1969, Nicholas Copernicus Award, Poland, 1972
His ashes lie in rest in the private Rockefeller family cemetery in Sleepy Hollow, New York.

In Closing -
What caused Chief Justice William Howard Taft just a year before his death to disregard his doctor's orders, that he might address a Psi Upsilon Convention? When his secretary remonstrated, he said: "This is for Psi U.; this is different." What brings the elect of the land hundreds of miles at considerable expense to our altar, just to renew the old bonds? It is the Psi U. Spirit"
Our fraternity is mindful of the memorable convention at Washington in May 1929, and of the great meeting in honor of Brother William Howard Taft at which he eloquently urged the mingled disciplines of scholarship and of character, of which he stood the best exemplar. We are mindful of his warnings against indifference and apathy, and of his satisfaction that Psi Upsilon had taken definite stand towards the recognition and betterment of academic work. To the initiative and leadership of our former Presidents and to the devotion of their successors and to the generosity of the donors of our scholarship awards, and to the sustained efforts of many in office and out of it, the fraternity and the colleges concerned are directly indebted.
